## What the paper claims

Theorem 1.1: there are absolute constants such that every tree of odd order
n >= n_0 with D vertices of degree two is edge-graceful whenever
n > C(D+1)(log n)^A. Theorem 1.2: with the linear deletion bound for cyclic
groups that Muyesser and Pokrovskiy state in their concluding section, the
threshold becomes linear, n > C(D+1).

## In scope: the four things repaired today

Attack these, and anything they touch.

### 1. Cancelling triples in the ledger

Section 3 lets a vertex cancel an odd number of active-child heads with a free
zero-sum triple. Such a triple is not closed under negation, so its negatives
now need a sink, and Appendix B counts it as a free unit. Questions:

- Is the count right? The manuscript says at most 2D/3 of them, because each
  consumes three owners and triples at different vertices consume disjoint sets
  of owners, and there are at most 2q <= 2D owners.
- Are there other special labels in the construction that are equally not closed
  under negation and still unaccounted for? Look in particular at what happens
  when a lone port is absorbed into a cancelled group (`port_into_group` in
  `context_scheduler.py`), and at the helper and recruited-port labels.
- The constructor registers these triples through a hook `after_cancel_group`
  (`free_token_constructor.py`, overridden in `sink_route/sink_constructor.py`).
  A witness certificate is at
  `data/sink_route/regression_cancel_triple/cert_sparse_n201_root17_seed7.json`.
  Note that whether a tree has such a triple depends on the root, and that the
  constructor's first root candidate usually has none.

### 2. Dense completion, Section 6

The proof of Lemma dense calls Lemma 6.23 of Muyesser-Pokrovskiy. It sets
epsilon = delta/24 and the deletion radius eta = delta/48, then claims both
input hypotheses hold and the output perturbation stays inside delta. Verify
every inequality in that paragraph, including the O(sqrt(n) log n) terms and the
bounded discrepancy |sum M^Q - sum M^R| <= 12. Say whether the constants close.
The previous version used eta = delta/8, which gave 3*epsilon*n on the input
side and did not satisfy the lemma's hypothesis; check that the new choice
really fixes that and does not break the output side.

### 3. Quad splitting

Remark rem:quadsplit says the negatives (q1,q2,q3,q4) of a free quad can go to
two triple sinks as {q1,q2,-q1-q2} and {q3,q4,-q3-q4}, that the two labels
introduced are an inverse pair, and that therefore every quad may be counted as
two triples, which makes cap (a count of triples) the right capacity for an
arbitrary mixture. Attack it: are the two blocks really zero-sum, is the pair
really inverse, do the new labels obey the magnitude bound, can they collide
with placed labels, and does the recount that follows hold arithmetically?
Recompute every number: t <= 9D, 7t <= 63D, m >= n/8 once n >= 286D+10, at most
127D+4 components, H >= m/2730 once n >= 7.0e5(D+1), paths >= t once
n >= 982800D, |S| <= 30D+3, and A = 1.9e8 with a placed-coefficient bound of 60.

### 4. The full-menu absorber gate

`data/sink_route/full_menu_gate.json` claims 2,175 parent families against
4,431 child families, 9,637,425 ordered pairs, no pair without a collision-free
role order, minimum 6 of 36, with the catalogue's SHA-256 recorded.
`full_menu_gate_verified.json` claims an independent verifier reproduces it,
histogram included. Check that the two implementations are genuinely
independent, that the pools are the right pools (is the parent/child predicate
the one Lemma compat actually needs?), and that passing this gate is what the
compatibility lemma requires rather than something weaker. Note that 59
catalogue families repeat a parameter name; those are distinct unknowns and must
be embedded by position, not by name.

Also check that the manuscript's numbers agree with the files.
