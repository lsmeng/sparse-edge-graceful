## Executive verdict

**The manuscript does not currently pass audit. The proofs as written do not
establish Theorems 1.1 or 1.2.**

This is not a counterexample to either theorem. No tree contradicting the
claimed conclusion was found. The supported conclusion is narrower: the
current manuscript contains several independent gaps in the route from the
finite catalogue to a labelling of every tree and then to dense completion.

The three principal gaps are:

1. Appendix A does not exhaust the owner configurations that can occur.
2. Appendix B's realization invariant is internally inconsistent, and its
   lattice/integrality and coefficient bounds are not established after
   carrier elimination.
3. Müyesser--Pokrovskiy's Lemma 6.24 does not directly supply the
   negation-paired collection of triples required by the dense-completion
   argument.

Consequently, both flagship theorems should remain `OPEN` until these bridges
are proved. The audit does not show that the theorems are false or
unrepairable.

## 1. Dependency chain and Müyesser--Pokrovskiy

The manuscript's proof chain is:

1. decompose the tree into owners, cells, arms, and ordinary blocks;
2. use Appendix A to assign a catalogue context to every owner;
3. select a family for every context and realize all special labels using
   Appendix B;
4. complete the unused labels into prescribed zero-sum ordinary blocks;
5. invoke the vertex-sum permutation lemma to obtain an edge-graceful
   labelling.

Theorem 1.1 invokes the printed perturbative results in MP Theorems 4.3--4.7
and the fixed-size zero-sum partition result in Lemma 6.24. The Section 7.4
remark is used only to replace the polylogarithmic deletion scale by a linear
scale in Theorem 1.2. Thus there is no evidence that the Section 7.4 remark is
secretly used in Theorem 1.1.

There is nevertheless a missing dependency. The manuscript must select one
zero-sum triple for every odd ordinary block while leaving a negation-stable
residue for inverse-pair filling. It asserts that, because the number of odd
blocks is even, the triples may be selected in pairs `T,-T`.

MP Lemma 6.24 supplies a partition of a suitable near-random set into
fixed-size zero-sum sets when the relevant divisibility condition holds. It
does not directly assert any of the following:

- that an arbitrary prescribed number of triples may be selected;
- that the selected triple matching is invariant under negation;
- that every selected triple occurs together with its negative.

The parity of the number of odd blocks alone does not imply this stronger
matching statement. A new symmetric matching lemma or a different completion
construction is required.

## 2. Appendix A is not exhaustive

Appendix A, Step 2 says that active children are cancelled until at most one
remains, except that exactly two may be retained for the portless residuals
`{1}` and `{2}` when no neutral unit is available. A child with forced head is
said to be paired with a sibling having a free head.

The following 19-vertex tree was independently supplied to the current
scheduler, rooted at vertex 3:

```text
(0,6) (1,17) (2,13) (6,15) (7,4) (9,3) (10,16)
(14,4) (4,3) (3,8) (15,11) (11,12) (12,8)
(17,16) (16,8) (8,13) (13,5) (5,18)
```

At vertex 8 the scheduler emits:

```text
context          h0_4_p0_act2
residual         {4}
ports            0
retained unit    none
active children  2
child types      L11, L11
```

Both L11 children are pass-through/forced-head types, so neither supplies the
free sibling required by Step 2. Retaining both children gives a two-input
owner with residual `{4}`, which is outside the manuscript's two-input
exception. Cancelling them instead leaves the Step 4 `{4}` case requiring a
neutral unit that is not present.

The implementation's `act2` state therefore covers a genuine configuration
that the written proof does not. This is a counterexample to the completeness
of the Appendix A case analysis, not a counterexample to the theorem itself.

## 3. Appendix B and the explicit constant

### 3.1 The charging bound

The combinatorial count can be reconstructed:

- subdivided-edge labels: at most `2D`;
- owner charges: at most `2D`;
- ports: at most `6D`;
- absorption charges: at most `4D`;
- root/bridge allowance: `3`.

This supports `|S| <= 12D+3`.

### 3.2 Contradictory state invariant

The declared state and invariant allow at most one pending token and at most
three live parameters. The later two-token-cell case explicitly allows two
pending tokens and four live carrier parameters. The final invariant check
nevertheless claims that the original one-token invariant follows from the
token cases. These statements cannot all hold simultaneously.

The current `FaithfulRealiser` implementation in fact stores a list of up to
two pending tokens, confirming that the executable state is not the state
proved in the appendix.

### 3.3 Missing algebraic implications

Several steps used in the realization proof do not follow from E1--E6:

- E3 says that recorded forms are globally nonzero and distinct; it does not
  imply that every label has nonzero slope in each coordinate when parameters
  are chosen one coordinate at a time.
- Rank two of the token map on a carrier does not imply that every other label
  involving that carrier remains nonconstant after the carrier is solved as an
  affine function of a previous carrier.
- Solving two rational affine equations does not automatically keep the new
  carrier in `(M Z)^2`.
- Inverting the carrier matrix can enlarge coefficients; denominators do not
  merely "decrease the magnitude."

For an actually selected family of context `h0_1-2_p1_act`, with carrier
parameters `[y,x]`, every nonsingular choice of two token coordinates has an
inverse coefficient of absolute value at least 2. Thus the catalogue's raw
coefficient bound 64 is not invariant under pairing substitution.

### 3.4 Arithmetic for A

The raw arithmetic in `numbers.tex` is internally consistent:

```text
coordinate linear term   36*29*24 = 25056
coordinate constant      24*(2*29 + 29^2 + 1) = 21600
pairing linear term       108*29*24 = 75168
pairing constant          24*(9*29^2 + 1) = 181680
raw magnitude bound       7*64*(75168+181680) = 115067904
rounded manuscript A      200000000
```

What fails is the premise that the post-elimination label coefficients are
still bounded by 64. Therefore the calculation agrees with `numbers.tex`, but
does not establish the stated constant.

## 4. `linear_route` propositions

All three proved cases survive independent algebraic checking.

1. For `s=0,1`, the identity permutation gives distinct even sums, while the
   reflection constant is odd.
2. For odd `t`, the stated rotation gives the claimed consecutive half-window
   at each endpoint.
3. In the composition formula, the internal rotation has sum interval
   `[(a-1)/2,(3a-3)/2]`, invariant under its internal reflection. The outer
   block indices inherit exactly the reflection condition of
   `FSP(b,s')`, with

   ```text
   s = a(s'-1) + (a+1)/2.
   ```

The proposition is correct.

The later consequence is not established: the FSP reduction treats the clean
tail `I=[1,M]`, while the actual special support produces a general punctured
set `I subsetneq [1,M]`. The manuscript itself admits that the general
punctured case has only been tested experimentally. Therefore even a complete
proof of the stated FSP conjecture would not yet prove the full dense lemma.

## 5. Closure checker

Running the bare command requested in the audit gives C1--C5 as passing, says
that C6 was not checked, and nevertheless exits successfully with:

```text
ALL CONDITIONS PASS
```

This output is misleading. Supplying the complete menu gate gives:

```text
menu families                         12743
all failing ordered context pairs      1973
emittable ordered pairs                11511
failures before encoded absorption        53
failures after encoded absorption          0
```

Thus the six encoded conditions close after the full gate is supplied. This
does not repair the mathematical completeness issue: the emittable-pair list
and absorption cases come from the same scheduler specification whose missing
`h0_4_p0_act2` case is identified above.

## 6. Alphabet-family checker

The advertised command prints 5,716 passing contexts. It loads only the
primary `family` for each context and ignores the additional `menu` entries,
so it does not execute the paper's claimed 12,743-family audit.

An independent loop over all menu entries found all 12,743 to pass the
properties actually implemented by the checker. The checker is substantially
independent of the CP-SAT search code: it rebuilds topology from the JSON
schema and does not import the searcher's verifier.

However, its condition set is incomplete:

- E6 is not checked.
- Its rank test includes the exposed head row in the token rank.
- It permits a weaker rank condition for root rows.
- Four menu alternatives declared as token-rank-two families have token forms
  of actual rank one:

```text
h2_1-6_p0_L1       menu 2
root_3-4_p0_bot    menu 2
root_5-4_p0_bot    menu 2
root_6_p2_L1       menu 2
```

Therefore the assertion that every catalogue family has E1--E6 is false as
currently certified.

The separate carrier file also does not give a family-level carrier for every
menu choice. Among the currently preferred context families with token and
carrier data, 107 carriers name parameters absent from the selected family and
55 have token rank below two on the specified carrier. Many remaining carrier
inverses are nonintegral.

## 7. Numerical reproduction

With the complete gate supplied, `paper_numbers.py` regenerates the checked-in
counts in `numbers.tex`, including:

```text
NumEmittable       2784
NumCatFamilies    12743
NumContexts        5716
NumPairs          11511
NumGateFail        1973
NumGateFailRealizable 53
AConst        200000000
```

Without the gate argument it writes gate counts `0/0`; the gate is not
automatically regenerated.

Constructor runs under Python 3.12 with OR-Tools reproduced:

```text
default mode     116/120
faithful mode     98/120
```

The faithful failures were 15 modular, 6 block-completion, and 1 bridge
failure in the fresh run. Its largest successful magnitude was 916, about
`101(D+1)`, rather than the paper's 692, about `76(D+1)`. The solver is not
configured to produce a fixed deterministic witness, and no archived
certificate identifies the run underlying 692.

Moreover, the aggregate for nominally successful faithful runs records 75
named lemma gaps, including third pending tokens and failures to pair two
pending tokens. A successful final labelling is therefore not the same thing
as a gap-free execution of the realization lemma.

The 116/120, 98/120, and maximum-magnitude figures are not generated by
`paper_numbers.py`, contrary to the blanket reproducibility claim.

## 8. Overstatements

At least the following statements require correction.

1. The `{1,3}` neutral-block search used at most three parameters and
   coefficients of absolute value at most 14. Failure in that finite search
   cannot establish that its labels cannot pay for themselves "however they
   are written."
2. A 5,716-primary-family run is described as checking all 12,743 catalogue
   families.
3. E6 is claimed for every family but is not checked by the cited checker.
4. The phrase "proof-faithful" suggests that successful runs follow the
   appendix, although successful runs themselves contain named lemma gaps.
5. The clean-tail FSP reduction is said to yield the complete dense lemma even
   though the general punctured case is explicitly left unreduced.

None of these finite-search statements should be upgraded to an unrestricted
impossibility or proof.

## 9. FSP through order 30

An independent exact CP-SAT model was run using Python 3.12 and OR-Tools with
two workers. Every integer shift in the stated window was checked for
`3 <= t <= 30`.

Results:

```text
feasible instances       474
infeasible instances       2
infeasible pairs          (t,s)=(4,-1),(4,2)
unknown/timeouts            0
```

This agrees with the manuscript.

The upper sharpness argument is valid. The manuscript does not explicitly
prove the lower side, but it follows from the reflection/complement transform
that sends `s` to `1-s`. This is a minor missing sentence, not a failure of the
finite claim.

## 10. Previous work

The previous result covers every odd-order tree with at most two degree-two
vertices, including small orders. The new claimed theorem would cover large
trees with arbitrarily many degree-two vertices subject to the sparse
inequality. Thus neither theorem contains the other as a complete statement.

The reformulation "equivalently, for every k >= 0" is not literally correct
at `k=0`: `n>2Ck` becomes `n>0`, whereas the original inequality still
requires `n>C` and the threshold `n>=n0`. It should be restricted to `k>=1`
or retain a bound proportional to `k+1`.

## 11. Literature audit

Direct comparison with the local full text supports the manuscript's
descriptions of:

- MP Lemma 6.24 as a fixed-size zero-sum partition result;
- MP Theorems 4.3--4.7 as providing the printed polylogarithmic perturbation
  scale;
- MP Theorem 5.11 as bounding commutator-product length logarithmically;
- Section 7.4 as stating that logarithms can be replaced by a term involving
  the commutator subgroup and noting a linear cyclic-group consequence.

The citation descriptions are therefore substantially accurate. The error is
the manuscript's inference from Lemma 6.24 to an arbitrary
negation-invariant partial triple matching.

Searches did not locate a prior theorem matching the claimed sparse-`D`
edge-graceful result. This is not a proof of priority. Older edge-graceful
literature is dispersed and was not exhaustively covered by subscription
indexes.

The exact owner-output neutral-adjunction lemma may be new, but direct-sum and
disjoint-variable gluing ideas are standard nearby constructions. Likewise,
the exact punctured Heffter problem was not found verbatim, but relative,
incomplete, and globally simple Heffter systems are close enough that novelty
should be stated cautiously.

## 12. Public archive

The Zenodo ZIP was downloaded and passed its advertised checksum and archive
integrity test. The main manuscript, source code, and principal catalogue data
are present.

Two reproducibility defects remain:

- `data/alphabet_carriers.json` is absent, although faithful constructor mode
  attempts to load it and silently falls back to an empty carrier mapping;
- the constructor certificates or result files underlying the reported
  116/120, 98/120, and magnitude statistics are absent.

The archive therefore does not fully reproduce the manuscript's faithful-mode
experiment without additional local data.

## Severity classification

### Severe: affects the claimed theorem

- Missing `h0_4_p0_act2` owner case in Appendix A.
- Contradictory pending-token invariant in Appendix B.
- Unproved nonconstancy, lattice divisibility, and coefficient control after
  carrier elimination.
- Missing symmetric `T,-T` triple-matching consequence in dense completion.
- Catalogue not certified to satisfy the complete E1--E6 interface.

### Medium: computation or statement must be corrected

- `closure_check.py` prints full success after skipping C6.
- The 5,716-context run is described as checking 12,743 families.
- The magnitude 692 is not reproduced by the current prescribed run.
- Required carrier data and constructor certificates are absent from Zenodo.
- Bounded search failure is stated as unrestricted impossibility.
- Successful faithful runs retain lemma gaps.

### Minor: wording or short omitted argument

- The lower side of the FSP sharpness window needs an explicit symmetry
  sentence.
- The `k=0` "equivalently" statement is false as written.
- `paper_numbers.py` needs the gate file explicitly to preserve gate counts.

## Confidence boundary and work not performed

The audit supports with high confidence that the current manuscript does not
prove its flagship theorems. It does not support the claim that either theorem
is false.

Verified or independently reproduced assets include:

- the combinatorial charge `|S| <= 12D+3`;
- the raw arithmetic in `numbers.tex`;
- the three proved FSP propositions;
- the finite FSP census through order 30;
- the default and faithful constructor success counts;
- the complete encoded gate after absorption.

The audit did not:

- hand-check all 12,743 catalogue families independently of their JSON
  representation;
- rerun all 30,442 original CP-SAT searches;
- reconstruct the full 375,600-state scheduler enumeration from first
  principles;
- prove a counterexample to either flagship theorem;
- exhaust MathSciNet, Zentralblatt, or every historical edge-graceful source.

The appropriate status is therefore:

```text
Theorem 1.1: OPEN -- proof gaps identified
Theorem 1.2: OPEN/CONDITIONAL -- same proof gaps plus the stated MP condition
Finite catalogue and FSP computations: useful evidence, not theorem proofs
```
